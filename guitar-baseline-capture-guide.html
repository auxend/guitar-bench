Luthier Bench Protocol Reverse-Engineering DNA Baseline

Capturing a Guitar's Baseline

When an instrument feels like pure magic, don't let seasonal changes or bad maintenance erase it. Here is the exact blueprint for documenting your "Holy Grail" setup.

01 The Philosophy of the "DNA Baseline"

Guitars are made of organic wood and under 100+ lbs of perpetual tension. When winter dry air or summer humidity hits, the neck moves. If you send it to an unfamiliar tech, they might "reset it to factory standard" and ruin the magic.

By capturing your guitar's baseline measurements, you achieve three things:

🛡️ Seasonal Immunity

When seasonal humidity changes bow your neck, a 5-minute 1/8th turn of the truss rod brings it exactly back to your documented baseline.

🧬 Cloning Magic

You can take the exact numbers from your "slammed" Jazzmaster and apply them directly to your Jaguar or Strats to give your entire collection the same effortless hand-feel.

🔧 Tech Runbook

Hand your tech the spec sheet: "Here is my exact relief, nut clearance, and 12th-fret action. Match these numbers." Zero room for guesswork.

02 The 7 Golden Measurements to Capture

Set the guitar in playing position (not laid flat on its back, as gravity pulls the neck into artificial relief). Record these 7 parameters:

Figure 1: Measurement Points Along the Neck Plane BENCH PROTOCOL
  1. 1. String Brand, Gauge & Concert Pitch

    Everything starts with tension. A set of 10–46 exerts ~103 lbs of pull; a set of 11–48 exerts ~125 lbs. Changing gauges changes the neck relief and break angle immediately. Note the exact gauge and tuning.

  2. 2. Neck Relief at the 8th Fret

    Put a capo on the 1st fret. Fret the Low E string with your finger at the 17th fret (where the neck meets the body). Measure the gap between the underside of the string and the top of the 8th fret wire.

    Pro tool: Feeler gauge (e.g. .004", .006", .010").
    Household check: Printer paper = ~.004"; standard business card = ~.012".

  3. 3. Nut Slot Clearance (Open & 3rd Fret Tap)

    Test A (The 3rd Fret Tap Test): Fret the string between the 2nd and 3rd fret. Tap the string above the 1st fret crown. Look for a microscopic whisper of daylight (.003"–.005"). If it's silent with zero movement, it's touching; if it has a big click, it's high.

    Test B (Open Gap): Measure the open string gap over the 1st fret crown for both Low E and High E (e.g. .014" Low E; .009" High E).

  4. 4. Action Height at the 12th & 17th Frets

    With the guitar tuned to pitch in playing position, measure from the top of the fret wire to the bottom of the string:

    - 12th Fret Low E & High E (Standard: 4/64" bass, 3/64" treble; Ultra-Low: 3/64" bass, 2/64" treble).
    - 17th Fret Low E & High E (Tells you if the neck pocket angle is correct or if action is rising).

  5. 5. Bridge Elevation & Saddle Arc

    Measure the physical distance from the body/pickguard to the bottom of the bridge plate. Use an understring radius gauge to check if the saddle curve matches the fingerboard (7.25", 9.5", or 12").

  6. 6. Neck Pocket Tilt & Shim Details

    Look at the neck pocket joint. Is the neck dead flat with the body, or is there an angled wedge shim (0.5° or 1.0°)? Note the presence and orientation of any shims.

  7. 7. Pickup Heights (Magnetic Pull Check)

    Depress the strings at the final fret (21st or 22nd). Measure the gap from the top of the pole pieces to the bottom of the string for both Neck and Bridge pickups. (If pickups are too close, magnets pull strings out of tune and choke sustain).

04 Your Guitar's Baseline Spec Sheet

Copy this format into your bench notes or workspace OpenSpec to preserve your Jazzmaster's DNA forever: 

# -------------------------------------------------------------
# GUITAR BASELINE DNA SHEET
# -------------------------------------------------------------
Guitar: "My Jazzmaster (Golden Reference)"
Date Documented: 2026-10-02
Scale Length: 25.5"
Tuning: E Standard (A=440Hz)
String Set: D'Addario EXL110 (10-46) or (11-48)

GEOMETRY:
- Relief at 8th Fret:   0.004" (1 sheet paper clearance)
- 1st Fret Nut Gap (E): 0.013" (Low E)
- 1st Fret Nut Gap (e): 0.009" (High E)
- 12th Fret Action (E): 3.0/64" (1.19mm)
- 12th Fret Action (e): 2.5/64" (0.99mm)
- 17th Fret Action (E): 3.2/64"
- Neck Shim Angle:      0.0° (Flat) or 0.5°
- Bridge Post Height:   Slammed (~1.5mm above pickguard)
- Saddle Type:          Mustang / Threaded / Staytrem

ELECTRONICS:
- Neck Pickup Gap (bass/treble):   3.0mm / 2.5mm
- Bridge Pickup Gap (bass/treble): 2.5mm / 2.0mm
# -------------------------------------------------------------
The Golden Takeaway

When you find a guitar that plays like a dream, the magic isn't a mystery—it's simply a collection of 5 to 7 specific mechanical clearances. Once you write them down, you own that feel forever.
